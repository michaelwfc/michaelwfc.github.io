You can easily import your photos and videos from your iPhone into macOS while preserving metadata—and when you import again later, only the new items will be added—by using the built-in Photos app. Here’s how to do it step by step:

1\. Prepare Your Devices

Unlock your iPhone. When you connect it to your Mac via a cable, tap “Trust This Computer” on your iPhone if prompted.

Use a good cable (or a reliable wireless option, if available) to ensure a smooth connection.

2\. Use the Photos App for Importing

The Photos app on macOS is designed to recognize which photos and videos have already been imported. When you launch it for a new import session, it will display only the items that have not been imported yet.

Step-by-Step Process

Connect Your iPhone to Your Mac

Plug your iPhone into your Mac using a USB cable.

Launch the Photos App

Open the Photos app (you can use Spotlight by pressing

⌘ + Space

and typing “Photos”).

If it doesn’t open automatically, it should detect your connected iPhone in the sidebar.

Go to the Import Tab

Switch to the Import view.

The app will scan your iPhone and display all photos and videos that haven’t been imported. Items already imported in a previous session are typically grayed out or omitted.

Import Your Files

Click “Import All New Photos” (or choose selected items and click “Import Selected”).

The Photos app will import the files along with all their metadata (such as the date, location, and any adjustments you made on the iPhone).

When Importing Again

The next time you connect your iPhone and open Photos, only the new photos and videos that haven’t yet been imported will show up in the import view. This way, you won’t create duplicates, and all your metadata is preserved with each import.

### Alternative: Use Image Capture

If you prefer a more manual approach or want additional control over the import process, you can use Image Capture, another macOS utility that preserves metadata.

Open Image Capture

You can find it in the Applications > Utilities folder or via Spotlight.

Select Your iPhone

Your iPhone should appear in the device list on the left side.

Choose a Destination Folder

Pick a folder on your Mac where you want the files saved.

Select the Files to Import

You can sort by file type (look for.MOV,.MP4, etc. for videos) and select only the files you need.

Import New Files Only

After importing, you can periodically check back; Image Capture will show all the files on your iPhone. You’ll need to manually choose new ones if you’re not using a method that automatically omits duplicates.

4\. Keeping Your Library Organized

Photos App Library: All your imported media remains organized in the Photos library, and you can create albums or use smart albums to further organize your pictures and videos.

Metadata Preservation: Both the Photos app and Image Capture maintain original metadata (such as exposure, geotags, and creation dates), so your files retain their information when imported.

Summary

Connect your iPhone and launch the Photos app.

Use the Import tab to see only the new media that hasn’t been imported before (the app automatically omits duplicates).

Alternatively, use Image Capture for a manual import process.

All original metadata is preserved when using these built-in apps.

This setup should allow you to frequently update your Mac with only the new photos and videos from your iPhone, keeping your library current without creating duplicates. Let me know if you have any more questions!

Alternatively, you can use the Windows Photos app:

Open the Photos App:

On your PC, open the Photos app. You can find it by searching for "Photos" in the Start menu.

Import from Your iPhone:

In the Photos app, click the "Import" button in the top-right corner.

Select "From a USB device."

The app will search for photos and videos on your iPhone. This may take a few moments.

Select Photos to Import:

Once the search is complete, you'll see a list of photos and videos on your iPhone.

Select the items you want to import, or click "Select all" to import everything.

Click "Import" to copy the selected photos and videos to your PC.

By following these steps, you should be able to transfer your photos from your iPhone to your Windows 10 PC easily.

To copy photos from an iPhone to a Windows 10 PC, follow these steps:

Connect Your iPhone to Your PC:

Use a USB cable to connect your iPhone to your Windows 10 PC.

If prompted, unlock your iPhone and tap "Trust" on the "Trust This Computer?" prompt.

Open File Explorer:

On your PC, open File Explorer. You can do this by pressing

Win + E

or clicking the File Explorer icon.

Locate Your iPhone:

In File Explorer, you should see your iPhone listed under "This PC" or "Devices and drives."

Click on your iPhone's name to open it.

Navigate to the DCIM Folder:

Inside your iPhone's directory, locate and open the "DCIM" folder. This folder contains all your photos and videos.

Copy Photos to Your PC:

Select the photos and videos you want to copy. You can use

Ctrl + A

to select all, or hold down

Ctrl

and click to select individual files.Right-click the selected files and choose "Copy," or press

Ctrl + C

.Navigate to the folder on your PC where you want to save the photos, right-click in the folder, and choose "Paste," or press

Ctrl + V
